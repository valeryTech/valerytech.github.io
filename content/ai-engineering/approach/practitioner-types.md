# Practical role taxonomy


This taxonomy groups AI roles by their work, outputs, and measures of success, beyond academic job titles.

<a id="1-a-minimal-set-of-axes-use-these-to-classify-any-ai-person"></a>

## 1. A minimal set of axes for classifying AI roles


**A. Research novelty (What is being optimized?)**

- **Discovery:** new knowledge / new methods (publishable novelty)
- **Adaptation:** known methods + new setting/data/constraints (measurable lift)
- **Delivery:** reliable product/infra outcomes (SLAs, cost, velocity)

**B. Object of work (What is the primary artifact?)**

- **Theory** (proofs, bounds)
- **Algorithms** (new objective, architecture, training trick)
- **Models** (weights, checkpoints, fine-tunes)
- **Systems** (training/inference stack, tooling)
- **Products** (user workflow, UX, business KPI)

**C. Time horizon**

- **Weeks:** experiments, prototypes, evaluations
- **Quarters:** production systems, platform capabilities
- **Years:** research programs, foundational bets

**D. Primary risk surface**

- **Scientific risk:** "won't work"
- **Engineering risk:** "won't scale / won't be reliable"
- **Product risk:** "nobody needs it"
- **Safety/compliance risk:** "harm / policy / legal exposure"

Role types can be defined as stable combinations of these axes.

## 2. Core archetypes (what they do, what they produce, how they're judged)

### A. Research-oriented roles


**1) Fundamental ML Researcher**

- **Mission:** produce novel methods/insights that generalize beyond a single product
- **Artifacts:** papers, theory notes, benchmark results, ablations, open problems
- **Core skills:** mathematical reasoning, experimental rigor, literature synthesis
- **Success metrics:** novelty + correctness, citations/impact, reproducible wins

**2) Applied Researcher (Problem-driven)**

- **Mission:** achieve step-function capability on a defined task/domain
- **Artifacts:** task framing, dataset specs, model variants, evaluation suites, reports
- **Core skills:** problem formulation, experimentation, error analysis, data strategy
- **Success metrics:** measurable lift vs baseline, robustness, transfer to productization

**3) Alignment / Safety Researcher**

- **Mission:** reduce misuse/harm and improve controllability/interpretability
- **Artifacts:** safety evals, red-team findings, mitigation proposals, policy guidance
- **Core skills:** threat modeling, eval design, human factors, adversarial thinking
- **Success metrics:** risk reduction demonstrated in evals, incident prevention, auditability

**4) Research Engineer (RE)**

- **Mission:** turn research ideas into fast, reliable experimentation and training runs
- **Artifacts:** training pipelines, experiment frameworks, reproducibility tooling, infra PRs
- **Core skills:** distributed training, profiling, systems engineering, testing discipline
- **Success metrics:** iteration speed, training stability, reproducibility, cost efficiency

### B. Model-and-data building roles (practitioner-heavy)


**5) Model Engineer (Training / Fine-tuning)**

- **Mission:** produce and improve model checkpoints (pretrain, fine-tune, distill)
- **Artifacts:** training recipes, checkpoints, hyperparam sweeps, model cards
- **Core skills:** optimization, scaling laws intuition, data curation signals, debugging training
- **Success metrics:** quality metrics, training success rate, cost per improvement, latency/size tradeoffs

**6) Data-Centric AI Engineer / Dataset Curator**

- **Mission:** make the data an engineered asset (coverage, labeling, governance)
- **Artifacts:** dataset versions, labeling guidelines, taxonomy/ontology, data QA checks
- **Core skills:** measurement design, sampling, labeling ops, bias/coverage analysis
- **Success metrics:** error reduction attributable to data fixes, dataset quality KPIs, lineage/compliance

**7) Evaluation Scientist / Evals Engineer**

- **Mission:** turn "model quality" into a testable contract; prevent regressions
- **Artifacts:** gold sets, synthetic sets, LLM-judge specs, scorecards, CI gates, dashboards
- **Core skills:** metric design, reliability/statistics, adversarial test construction
- **Success metrics:** correlation with user outcomes, regression catch rate, low flakiness, decision usefulness

### C. Product delivery roles (LLM apps, workflows, experiences)


**8) AI Product Engineer (Full-stack with AI core)**

- **Mission:** ship user-facing workflows using models (LLM features, copilots, agents)
- **Artifacts:** product endpoints, UI flows, prompt/tool schemas, analytics instrumentation
- **Core skills:** software engineering + LLM patterns (RAG, tools, guardrails), product sense
- **Success metrics:** adoption, task success rate, retention, latency/cost, incident rate

**9) Applied LLM Engineer (RAG / Tools / Agents)**

- **Mission:** design and harden LLM workflows (retrieval, tool use, planning, memory)
- **Artifacts:** retrieval pipelines, tool contracts, prompts, orchestration logic, failure-mode playbooks
- **Core skills:** prompt/program co-design, retrieval & ranking, grounding, controllability
- **Success metrics:** groundedness, tool success rate, reduced hallucinations, stable behavior under distribution shift

**10) Conversational UX / AI Interaction Designer (specialist)**

- **Mission:** make AI behavior legible, steerable, and aligned with user intent
- **Artifacts:** conversation patterns, clarifying-question policies, tone guides, fallback designs
- **Core skills:** dialogue design, cognitive load management, evaluation with humans
- **Success metrics:** fewer dead-ends/escalations, higher task completion, lower user confusion

### D. Platform and operations roles (make AI shippable at scale)


**11) AI Platform Engineer / MLOps Engineer**

- **Mission:** provide reliable pipelines for training, deployment, monitoring, rollback
- **Artifacts:** model registry, feature store, deployment tooling, observability stack, incident runbooks
- **Core skills:** infra, SRE practices, CI/CD, monitoring, cost controls
- **Success metrics:** uptime/SLOs, deploy frequency, mean time to recovery, cost per request/train

**12) Inference / Serving Engineer**

- **Mission:** make inference fast, cheap, and predictable
- **Artifacts:** serving stack, caching/batching, quantization plans, throughput benchmarks
- **Core skills:** performance engineering, GPU/accelerator stacks, systems profiling
- **Success metrics:** p95 latency, throughput, cost, tail stability, availability

**13) Responsible AI / Governance Lead (practitioner but cross-functional)**

- **Mission:** ensure compliance, safety processes, auditability, and policy alignment
- **Artifacts:** risk assessments, approval workflows, model documentation, audit trails
- **Core skills:** risk management, policy interpretation, measurement + controls
- **Success metrics:** audit pass rate, incident reduction, time-to-approval without chaos

### E. Leadership and integrator roles


**14) AI Architect / Tech Lead**

- **Mission:** choose the right system design (model + data + eval + infra + product)
- **Artifacts:** architecture docs, interfaces, NFRs, roadmap, make/buy decisions
- **Core skills:** systems thinking, tradeoff analysis, cross-team alignment
- **Success metrics:** delivery predictability, avoided rework, sustainable quality/cost envelope

**15) AI Product Manager (AI PM)**

- **Mission:** define what "good" means and prioritize the work that moves outcomes
- **Artifacts:** PRDs, KPI trees, launch criteria, user research synthesis, risk plans
- **Core skills:** problem framing, metric design, stakeholder management
- **Success metrics:** shipped outcomes, KPI movement, clarity of scope, stakeholder alignment

## 3. A compact reusable map


Think of a 2D map:

- **X-axis:** Discovery -> Adaptation -> Delivery
- **Y-axis:** Model/Theory -> Systems -> Product

Then:

- Fundamental researchers sit at **Discovery + Model/Theory**
- Applied researchers and model engineers sit at **Adaptation + Model**
- RE / platform / serving sit at **Delivery + Systems**
- LLM product engineers sit at **Delivery + Product**
- Evals spans **Adaptation/Delivery** across **Model -> Product** (it is the connective tissue)

## 4. How to apply this structure (quick method)


When a title is ambiguous (for example, "AI Engineer" or "Research Scientist"), classify the role by:

1. **Primary artifact shipped** (paper, checkpoint, eval suite, platform capability, product feature)
2. **Primary success metric** (novelty, lift, reliability/cost, adoption)
3. **Primary risk they own** (scientific, engineering, product, safety)

That yields a stable "type" even when org titles are inconsistent.

Possible extension: a rubric for classifying people and roles in a team, including the expected interfaces between model, evaluation, product, and platform work.
