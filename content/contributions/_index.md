## How the contributions inform one another


The workspace discussion starts with a concern about how an interface can constrain thought. Composable operations make alternative investigations possible; that raises a further question about what those operations preserve. Evidence, unresolved assumptions, and the meaning of a result matter when work is combined.

The evaluation model makes some of those distinctions explicit. It separates what happened from what was recorded, and what was recorded from what can be judged. The product notes then ask how a supported finding should change the next investigation, the interaction, the implementation, or the commitment made to users.

This relationship crosses interaction design, architecture, and product judgment. ## Reading and assessing a contribution


Start with the problem and the decision the contribution changes. Follow the cited passage before making a claim about its scope or authorship. A recorded author intervention, a subsequent model proposal, and a later editorial interpretation can all be useful while playing different roles.

Assess conceptual reasoning, implemented mechanisms, demonstrated outcomes, and comparative originality separately. Evidence for one does not automatically settle the others. Comparisons should name the relevant work and explain both overlap and difference. A specialist comparison for one article answers a different question from a comparison of one person's connected body of work.
