# Aligning on AI product engineering


I want to build useful AI products, take responsibility for how they behave in production, and help improve the practice used to develop them. I’m looking for work where I can apply what I have learned, continue developing my judgment, and help a team improve how it works.

This note explains what that contribution involves and what I would need to establish with a company considering working with me.

## A practice still taking shape


I see AI product engineering as a practice still taking shape. It combines software and systems engineering, knowledge of model behavior, experimentation, product work, and domain expertise. We have established knowledge to use in each area. How we bring it together into a dependable way of developing AI products still needs examination.

My concern is that people can talk about the practice as if those questions were already settled. Saying “we have AI engineers” or “we do evals” leaves open how the team understands the system, judges its behavior, and decides what users can rely on.

A clear product goal can coexist with uncertainty about whether and how a proposed system can achieve it. The team may need to investigate the implementation while also examining whether its methods give it a sound basis for judging progress.

I expect a scientific mindset: distinguish observations from assumptions, investigate important questions, and revise conclusions when evidence calls for it. Shared language helps people examine what is being claimed. Discipline makes that examination part of everyday engineering work. My own approach must remain open to the same scrutiny.

I develop this argument in [Language, mindset, and discipline in AI product development]({{< ref "ai/approach/minset-language-discipline" >}}).

## The problems I want to help address


In AI product work, I have seen claims about reliability or readiness exceed the evidence behind them. A team can demonstrate useful behavior while leaving important questions about supported use unanswered.

An evaluation score may improve without establishing whether the product helps users complete their work. A prototype may become something people depend on before the team has agreed on its limits and operating responsibilities. Findings may reveal a problem while the decisions that depend on it remain difficult to revisit.

These gaps affect practical company decisions: which solution to pursue, what to offer users, where to invest engineering time, and what to change next. I want to help connect those decisions to an understanding of the product’s behavior, its usefulness, and the work required to support it.

Evaluation is one foundation for doing this. It helps us examine what the system does, compare changes, investigate failures, and discover ways to improve or extend the product. A continuing evaluation capability includes people, methods, data, and software, with shared criteria that make findings understandable across roles.

Evaluation provides evidence for decisions. The surrounding engineering work must implement changes, enforce requirements, and operate the product. Understanding whether the product helps people also requires knowledge of users and their workflow.

[The role of evaluation in AI product development]({{< ref "ai/approach/evaluations" >}}) explains why I treat this capability as part of building and delivering the product.

## The contribution I want to make


My approach has developed through building systems, observing their behavior, collecting evidence, and reflecting on decisions. I want that experience to influence how problems are framed, which assumptions receive attention, what we investigate, and how we interpret the results.

I expect that experience to be examined through concrete work: what was uncertain, what I did, what happened, and which decisions changed. A description of my approach explains how I currently think; examples from the work establish the experience behind it.

I want to contribute across implementation, architecture, evaluation, and production operation, working with people who understand the users and the domain. [My perspective on production AI engineering](https://valery.tech/ai/approach/my-perspective/) describes that scope and the surrounding engineering system in more detail.

Part of this contribution can be helping a team identify needs it has not yet made explicit. Examining actual behavior together can reveal why an existing measurement is insufficient or why a recurring question needs better support. It can also show that my initial diagnosis was incomplete or that a smaller change would address the problem.

I also want the work to develop my judgment further. That means substantive questions I have not already answered, access to real use and production experience, and colleagues whose knowledge can challenge and improve mine. I want to develop both the product and the methods we use to understand it.

## The conditions the work requires


A company does not need to arrive with a finished AI engineering practice or use my terminology. Establishing missing methods and capabilities can be part of the role. I look at what the team can currently do, how it responds to gaps in its understanding, and whether it can support the work needed to address them.

That support needs to be practical. It includes access to users, domain experts, relevant data, and production evidence. It includes time and engineering capacity for investigation and for maintaining the practices the product depends on. It also requires clarity about how findings can affect scope, architecture, releases, and product expectations.

I expect questions about relevance, cost, consequences, and alternatives. Those questions can improve the proposal. Understanding a concern, agreeing on its priority, and committing resources to address it are separate steps. What I need is a discussion that can progress through them.

Helping establish the need is work I am willing to do. Once that need becomes a continuing dependency of product development, responsibility for it must be shared and supported. The role should give me room to do the engineering, rather than leave me continually supplying it through personal time.

This still means delivering. I expect us to choose investigations that support the decisions ahead, keep the amount of process appropriate to the consequences, and make product commitments we can support. Discovery and delivery can continue together as we learn from implementation and use.

## What we need to agree on


For me, alignment means a shared understanding of the actual work: what the company wants to accomplish, what I would be responsible for, which practices need development, and what support that requires. It also means understanding who makes the relevant decisions and how my contribution can influence them.

That understanding should develop through discussing the company’s problems and concrete examples from my experience. The company needs to assess whether my judgment is useful for its work. I need to understand whether the project, the people, and the role will let me apply and develop it. We should both be able to question and revise our initial interpretation.

Some conditions may already exist; others may need to be established. Those differences should be explicit. Existing access to domain experts, for example, is a different starting point from an intention to arrange it later. Agreement should identify what still needs to happen and who will support it.

I am willing to help develop the environment in which this work can succeed. I want us to agree that investigating the product and improving the practice are supported parts of the responsibility to build and deliver it.
