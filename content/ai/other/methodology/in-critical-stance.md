Several related terms emphasize different aspects of evidence-based practice:

- **Empirical validation** -- checking whether a claim holds up against observation or experience.
- **Reflective practice** -- acting, then systematically reflecting on events and lessons learned.
- **Experiential learning** -- learning through experience, reflection, forming a new understanding, and trying again.
- **Action research** -- a more formal cycle of trying something in practice, collecting evidence, reflecting, and adjusting.

## evidence-based engineering with critical reflection


**Empirical software engineering** systematically tests engineering claims using observation and experiments.

**Evidence-based software engineering** follows the principle that decisions and practices should be supported by the best available evidence.

**Hypothesis-driven engineering** turns claims into explicit, falsifiable hypotheses and experiments.

**Experimental evaluation** compares an approach against baselines and measures outcomes.

- **Critical evaluation** -- examine claims, assumptions, evidence, limitations, and failure cases.
- **Critical inquiry** -- broader; question claims, investigate them, test them, and revise conclusions.
- **Critical empirical practice** -- combines skepticism with testing ideas against observed evidence.
- **Critical engineering practice** -- a description of an engineering culture, though not a standard formal term.
- **Critical evidence-based practice** -- emphasizes both evidence and scrutiny of the evidence itself.
- **critical empirical practice** suggests a loop such as:

> **question -> make the claim explicit -> test it -> inspect the evidence -> challenge the interpretation -> reflect -> revise**

That last part is important. Pure "empirical evaluation" can become the assumption that measuring a metric establishes an answer. A **critical empirical** approach also asks whether the metric was appropriate, whether the experiment was biased, what assumptions were hidden, what cases were excluded, and whether the conclusion actually follows from the evidence.

An engineering practice where claims, assumptions, and proposed solutions are systematically questioned, tested against evidence, and revised based on observed results.

## Stance


**Critical stance**: examining claims, practices, and expert opinions through their assumptions, evidence, incentives, and failure modes.

**Empirical stance**: checking claims against observation, experiments, or actual practice.

**Critical empirical stance** combines these dispositions:

> A disposition to question claims and assumptions, test them against evidence and practice, and revise conclusions based on observed results.

**Critical empirical stance** names the mindset; **critical empirical practice** names the process of testing, evaluating, and reflecting.
