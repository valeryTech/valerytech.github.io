The capability notes conclude that "the model can reason" or "the model understands" is not an observation. It is a hypothesis about a hidden property, and an empirical study has to test it. [05]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/05-measuring-dispositions" >}}) sets out what to measure. It does not say how to run the study: how to state the claim, preregister the measures, control the comparison, validate the scorer, appraise the evidence, and report it.

Much of that method already exists in notes written for other purposes: appraising claims about coding agents, comparing versions of an AI system, investigating failed customer tasks, discovering what an agent can do in a sandbox, evaluating design artifacts. This note finds those notes, lines up the concepts they share with the capability notes, and marks where the parallels hold and where they break.

The correspondences in the tables come from the notes themselves. The interpretive links (the ones that say *X is the same move as Y*) are this map's reading and are marked as such where they go beyond what either note says.

## Correspondences

### 1. Every cluster separates levels of claim


The inference ladder has seven rungs, from behavior to transported claim. [13]({{< ref "ai/design-research/06-research-reports/13-research-method-and-claim-validation" >}}) proposes six levels: observation, operational result, construct inference, mechanism, generalization, forecast. The [causal stack]({{< ref "ai-engineering/causal-stack" >}}) keeps observation, possible explanation, proposed change, and customer outcome separate. The [evidence protocol]({{< ref "ai-engineering/evaluation/theoretical-methodology/appraisal/evidence-protocol" >}}) says "Lead time dropped 18%" is evidence and "the agent understands the architecture" is an interpretation needing separate evidence. [Conceptualization]({{< ref "ai-engineering/evaluation/framework/conceptualization" >}}) splits what evaluation can establish (what behavior, under which conditions, how often) from what diagnosis must investigate (which mechanism produced it). The [experimentation primer]({{< ref "ai-engineering/agents/agents-md/coding-agent-experimentation-primer" >}}) types research questions as descriptive, comparative or causal, explanatory, and design-oriented, which is [05]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/05-measuring-dispositions" >}})'s three levels plus the design use of [08]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/08-deriving-capabilities-from-tasks" >}}).

The rules are the same: each level needs its own evidence, and evidence for a lower level does not carry up. The levels themselves differ. 13 and the ladder go up toward a latent property. The causal stack goes sideways, toward a change and its effect on the customer. A complete capability study needs the vertical chain. A product investigation needs the sideways one.

### 2. "Productivity" plays the role of "reasoning"


[Measures and constructs]({{< ref "engineering/evidence/measures-and-constructs" >}}) says this directly: "engineering productivity" is a theoretical construct, and merged-PR count is one operationalization of it whose validity must be argued. That is [01]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/01-what-is-a-capability-claim" >}})'s argument with "reasoning" for "productivity" and benchmark accuracy for PR count. The protocol's Goodhart example (split work into agent-friendly PRs and the count goes up) is a mimic in the sense of [03]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/03-what-a-disposition-claim-means" >}}): the right score by the wrong route.

This gives the capability notes a second worked case. The EBSE notes give the capability notes a procedure, and the capability notes give the EBSE notes a theory of what the construct is.

### 3. The sandbox-first capability record is the response-profile account in engineering form


This is the closest match among these notes. [Sandbox-first capability discovery]({{< ref "ai/design/workflow-design/sandbox-first-capability-discovery-llm-agents" >}}) says a capability should be recorded as

> Model **M**, with environment **E**, tools **T**, instructions **P** and budget **B**, succeeds on task class **C** with reliability **R**.

and that sandbox experiments discover "observed capabilities under known conditions", never "all possible capabilities". Read against [04]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/04-dispositions-as-response-profiles" >}}): \(M, E, T, P, B\) are the system boundary \(S\); task class \(C\) is the declared scenario family; reliability \(R\) is the summary \(\Phi\) and the threshold \(\tau\); "observed under known conditions" is the inference from limited evidence (feature 8). The note's two rules, "failure does not establish inability; success does not establish reliability", are the mask and the reliability problem. [Model and system behavior]({{< ref "ai-engineering/causal-stack/reference/model-and-system" >}}) states the first rule for product work: do not infer "a competence boundary from one failure". [Working assumptions]({{< ref "ai-engineering/working-assumptions" >}}) states the claim form: an intelligence claim "is incomplete unless it identifies the system, task or task distribution, operating conditions, and evaluation".

What the sandbox note lacks is the response curve. It records success on a task class at one reliability. The capability notes would vary a property of the task class and record how reliability changes. The METR task-horizon result the sandbox note cites ("capability changes sharply with task horizon") is exactly such a curve, with horizon as \(\pi\).

### 4. The accepted form of a claim is a disposition claim with declared conditions


[Foundations]({{< ref "ai-engineering/evaluation/theoretical-methodology/framework/foundations-v1-3" >}}) accepts only claims of the form "Variant B is better than A on endpoint E in regime(s) R, within constraints, with uncertainty", and defines a regime as a condition under which behavior differs materially: input mix, tool health, judge version. [03]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/03-what-a-disposition-claim-means" >}}) makes the same demand of "x is disposed to M when C": the conditions \(C^*\) have to be stated or the claim is empty. Both say that a claim without its range is not a claim. [04]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/04-dispositions-as-response-profiles" >}})'s open question "what persists across scenarios?" is the methodology framework's comparability invariant: a delta is uninterpretable if configuration, inputs, environment, and ruler all move at once.

### 5. Finks, masks, and mimics are validity threats


This is this map's reading. The three counterexamples from the philosophy of dispositions match named threats in the engineering notes, and the engineering notes have many concrete instances of each.

| Counterexample ([03]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/03-what-a-disposition-claim-means" >}})) | Engineering threat | Instances in the notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mimic**: the right output by the wrong route | construct-validity threat, Goodhart, ruler drift (T2) | "a manually rescued workflow can look like successful automation" ([cases]({{< ref "ai-engineering/causal-stack/cases" >}})); "emitting plan-shaped language is not the same thing as carrying out model-based planning" (ReAct report); literal-overlap shortcuts in long-context tests, weak tests inflating code correctness, contamination ([perception]({{< ref "ai-engineering/agents/perception-and-context-management" >}}), [data]({{< ref "ai-engineering/delivery/df-data" >}})); perturbation checks "to detect a program that merely memorized" ([09]({{< ref "ai/design-research/01-foundations/09-verification-and-acceptance" >}})); "a common outcome is assumed to imply a common mechanism" (knowledge loop) |
| **Mask**: circumstances block a disposition that is present | environmental confounding by tools, retrieval, or provider (T3). The system boundary moved. | "unavailable or omitted information versus incorrect output despite supplied information" ([debugging]({{< ref "ai-engineering/causal-stack/debugging" >}})); "a budget limit silently changes the result" ([interventions]({{< ref "ai-engineering/causal-stack/reference/interventions" >}})); unnecessary refusal judged alongside wrong answers ([checks]({{< ref "ai-engineering/causal-stack/checks" >}})); a retrieval failure "indistinguishable from one that retrieved perfectly but then compressed away the critical detail", and claimed context size overstating usable context ([perception]({{< ref "ai-engineering/agents/perception-and-context-management" >}})); RQ6, telling "missing knowledge from failed retrieval, ignored instructions, ambiguous requirements, and capability limits" ([research questions]({{< ref "ai-engineering/agents/agents-md/coding-agent-research-questions" >}})) |
| **Fink**: probing changes the property | reactivity, evaluation awareness. In EBSE, the skill factor: outcomes depend on who uses the tool. | almost absent. The nearest: "evidence available only to the evaluator must not silently change participant behavior" ([evidence model]({{< ref "ai-engineering/evaluation/framework/evidence-model" >}})); feedback changing future inputs ([model and system]({{< ref "ai-engineering/causal-stack/reference/model-and-system" >}})) |
| **Implicit conditions**: the speaker's \(C^*\) is left unstated | input mix shift and Simpson's paradox (T1), comparability invariants | "a different document set can make the task easier or harder" (review flow); "a change in an evaluation method can also look like a change in product quality" ([evaluations]({{< ref "ai-engineering/approach/evaluations" >}})) |

The match is close for mimics and masks. It is loose for finks. A fink changes the system, while the EBSE skill factor changes the person using it. Both make the result depend on the act of measuring, but for different reasons. The engineering notes have no concept of evaluation awareness, which is the fink that matters most for frontier models.

RQ6 in the research questions is worth singling out. Its list (missing knowledge, failed retrieval, ignored instructions, ambiguous requirements, capability limits) is a taxonomy of what a single failure might be, and only the last item is a claim about the disposition. The capability notes say this abstractly; the research question says it for a coding agent.

### 6. The scorer is itself an instrument to validate


The capability notes put the scoring rule at rung 2 and say little about validating it. The engineering notes say a great deal, and it transfers.

- The methodology framework's assumption A3: "measurement is an instrument, not ground truth". The ruler produces \(m = r(x, \tau, y)\), possibly noisy, and "ruler unhealthy" makes a result inconclusive.
- [Judgment and findings]({{< ref "ai-engineering/evaluation/framework/30-judgment-and-findings" >}}) validates an evaluator on clear passes and failures, boundary cases, known evaluator mistakes, and cases where evidence is insufficient, and checks "stability across repeated runs" and "sensitivity to irrelevant wording or ordering". Those are the reliability and the mask problem applied to the judge. "Repeated adjustment against the same cases can make the evaluator appear more reliable than it is" is Goodhart applied to the judge.
- "There is no universal acceptable evaluator score. The required reliability depends on the consequence of an incorrect judgment." That is the threshold \(\tau\) of [04]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/04-dispositions-as-response-profiles" >}}), relative to the inquiry \(q\), applied to the instrument.
- [A reference example is not ground truth]({{< ref "ai/product/operating-model/reference-example-is-not-ground-truth" >}}) reserves "ground truth" for values set by an independent source, which is the independence requirement of step 3 in [05]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/05-measuring-dispositions" >}}).
- Eval failure modes: at a 5% failure rate an always-pass judge has 95% agreement and zero failure recall. Agreement is not validity.
- The [uncertainty ladder]({{< ref "ai/product/discovery/uncertainty-ladder" >}}): "the measurement is itself on the ladder", and an evaluator's possibility closes only by validation against trusted judgments on held-out cases including boundary cases.

A capability study that uses a model judge needs all of this before rung 2 can be trusted.

### 7. Which set the number is over


[04]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/04-dispositions-as-response-profiles" >}}) leaves open "what measure over scenarios?": physical chance, a reference population, the distribution of an experimental design, expected operating conditions. [Judgment and findings]({{< ref "ai-engineering/evaluation/framework/30-judgment-and-findings" >}}) answers it pragmatically with a table of evidence sources: a coverage or challenge set supports claims about the represented situations, a regression set about protected behavior, a representative production sample an estimate for that population and period. "Do not interpret a challenge-set rate as an estimate of normal production quality." [Evaluations]({{< ref "ai-engineering/approach/evaluations" >}}) says the same: "a deliberately difficult case can demonstrate that a failure is possible. It does not establish how frequently that failure occurs in production." In capability terms, a challenge set gives an existence result (the possibility rung) and a production sample gives a frequency under one \(\mu_q\); neither is the response profile, which needs the demand varied on purpose.

### 8. Demand curves exist in fragments


The capability notes' central object, \(\hat p(v \mid \pi, \theta)\) over a controlled range of \(\pi\), does not appear as such anywhere in the engineering notes. Pieces of it do:

- [User inputs]({{< ref "ai-engineering/evaluation/framework/10-user-inputs" >}}): "start with clear and feasible requests to establish baseline behavior", then add ambiguity, missing information, conflicting requirements. This is stepwise increase of demand, without a curve.
- The [ReAct report]({{< ref "ai-engineering/agents/react/historical-critique" >}}): "performance falls sharply as environments become stateful, multimodal, policy-constrained, or long-horizon". Four demand dimensions, named but not scaled.
- [Data]({{< ref "ai-engineering/delivery/df-data" >}}) slices by "task complexity" and "input-complexity bands". RAG metrics says an aggregate "is almost meaningless unless segmented" and segments by difficulty, intent, and number of sources required.
- The [utility ladder]({{< ref "ai/product/operating-model/solution-utility-ladder" >}})'s U4, "holds across variation", names the range without measuring across it.

Two engineering rules guard the circularity that [05]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/05-measuring-dispositions" >}}) warns about: a dimension "must describe something identifiable from the user's input itself" (user inputs), and "evaluation cases should represent product requirements rather than being written around one model's capabilities" (data). Both define the demand independently of the model. The same user-inputs note, however, picks its dimensions from where failures were observed, which is the seed-from-failure move of correspondence 10 and needs the independence rule to stay honest.

### 9. Coverage, repetition, and reliability are three different things


[Checks]({{< ref "ai-engineering/causal-stack/checks" >}}): "more repetitions of a few cases do not establish coverage of new cases." [Practical checks]({{< ref "ai-engineering/causal-stack/learning-from-real-use" >}}): repeated generations "reveal variation within a task; they do not create new independent tasks or customers". The sandbox note and the ReAct report both use pass^k, success on all \(k\) repeats, as a strict reliability measure. [Prompt-driven]({{< ref "ai/design/prompt-driven" >}}): "one successful run shows that the system worked once".

In [04]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/04-dispositions-as-response-profiles" >}})'s terms, repetition estimates the stochastic model \(\mathcal{M}_q\) at one point of \(\pi\); coverage is the extent of the scenario family; reliability is a feature \(\Phi\) of the profile. The engineering notes keep them apart without the notation. [Design pressure]({{< ref "engineering/eng-exp/design-pressure" >}}) pulls the other way: it wants CI gates "stable and deterministic", which hides variance instead of measuring it.

### 10. Failure analysis is a source of hypotheses about \(\pi\)


[05]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/05-measuring-dispositions" >}}) says that for AI systems, finding the contextual properties \(\pi\) is part of the research. [Failure understanding]({{< ref "ai-engineering/evaluation/framework/20-error-analysis" >}}) builds failure categories bottom-up from traces, by open coding, focused coding, and taxonomy, and keeps cause hypotheses separate.

This is this map's reading. A failure category is a candidate demand: "fails when the answer depends on a value three turns back" names a property of the context that can be operationalized and varied. Failure analysis tells you where to look. Dispositional measurement tests whether that dimension really drives the behavior. The evidence model's failure model, "properties and variation, observed conditions, relationships between failures", is the nearest engineering analogue to a nomological network, though it is a network of failures rather than of a construct.

### 11. Circular difficulty is the forking-paths problem


[05]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/05-measuring-dispositions" >}}) rejects difficulty inferred from failure rates as circular. The [evidence protocol]({{< ref "ai-engineering/evaluation/theoretical-methodology/appraisal/evidence-protocol" >}}) requires metrics to be defined before looking at results, because otherwise "it's extremely easy to find a metric that moved". [13]({{< ref "ai/design-research/06-research-reports/13-research-method-and-claim-validation" >}}) recommends preregistration and Registered Reports. The [experimental design]({{< ref "ai-engineering/evaluation/theoretical-methodology/framework/experimental-design-v1-1" >}}) spec requires precommitted endpoints. [Data]({{< ref "ai-engineering/delivery/df-data" >}}) names the slow version: "AI teams frequently optimise against the same examples they use to claim quality." In each case the protection is the same: fix the variables and the analysis independently of the outcome, and keep the cases used to invent a fix apart from the cases used to assess it ([cases]({{< ref "ai-engineering/causal-stack/cases" >}}), [retrieval design]({{< ref "ai-engineering/context/retrieval/retrieval-design" >}})).

### 12. Mechanism claims need interventions, and may be left open


The mechanistic level of [05]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/05-measuring-dispositions" >}}) and the component interventions in [08]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/08-deriving-capabilities-from-tasks" >}}) §6 (remove the dependency graph, replace it with an oracle, corrupt it) are what the protocol and 13 call ablation, what the [methodology principles]({{< ref "ai-engineering/evaluation/theoretical-methodology/framework/methodology-principles" >}}) call designing for attribution, and what the sandbox note's experiments table calls component ablation (add a planner, reviewer, or memory one at a time). The [ReAct report]({{< ref "ai-engineering/agents/react/historical-critique" >}}) gives the rule for a fair comparison: ask "whether gains come from architecture, additional inference compute, a better base model, extra information, or benchmark-specific tuning", and hold the base model, tools, demonstrations, token budget, and retries fixed. It also asks the mechanistic question in its sharpest form: a trace of thoughts is not an explanation until an intervention on the trace is shown to change the decision.

13 adds the step the capability notes lack: write at least two competing explanations before the experiment, and design it to tell them apart. [Claims]({{< ref "ai-engineering/causal-stack/claims" >}}) adds the stopping rule: "if knowing the internal mechanism would not change the product decision, leave that question open." That agrees with 05, which says the mechanistic level is not required for a disposition claim.

### 13. Three ladders for one claim


This is this map's reading. Three notes put a claim on a ladder, and they measure different things.

| Ladder | Asks | Rungs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Inference ladder]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/00-the-inference-ladder" >}}) | What is inferred in saying a system can do something? | behavior, score, system boundary, response profile, disposition, construct label, transported claim |
| [Uncertainty ladder]({{< ref "ai/product/discovery/uncertainty-ladder" >}}) | What is unknown about getting it to work? | possibility, approach, feasibility, operation, value |
| [Solution utility ladder]({{< ref "ai/product/operating-model/solution-utility-ladder" >}}) | What does the product claim to do for the user? | U1 valid output through U7 a changed target condition; "evidence for one does not establish the others" |

They line up loosely. The uncertainty ladder's possibility rung is the weakest "can": the disposition shows up for some \(\pi\) and some boundary \(S\), under the most favorable conditions and with hand-steering allowed, which is an anti-mask move. Its approach rung fixes the boundary: the scaffolding becomes part of \(S\) ([04]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/04-dispositions-as-response-profiles" >}}) feature 3). Its feasibility rung is the profile over the declared range of \(\pi\), including where it stops holding. Operation is the transported claim. Value is criterion validity in use. The utility ladder's U4, "holds across variation", is the range of \(\pi\); its "utility is not coverage" is the distinction between \(\Phi\) and the scenario family. All three say to ask per claim, not per system. The uncertainty ladder's per-claim record (claim, situation, bar) is close to the capability notes' triple of system, scenario family, and threshold \(\tau\), and its observation that "capabilities are jagged" is the claim that the response profile is not monotone in any obvious variable.

### 14. Artificial and naturalistic evaluation divide the ladder


[Design-science evaluation]({{< ref "ai-engineering/delivery/design-science-evaluation" >}}) says artificial evaluations give high internal validity and naturalistic ones give high external validity. In inference-ladder terms, controlled measurement can establish rungs 4 and 5 (profile and disposition) for a declared range. Rung 7 (transport) needs naturalistic evidence. [Production learning]({{< ref "ai-engineering/evaluation/framework/production-learning" >}}) and the causal stack's "observe continued product use" are where that evidence comes from in product work.

### 15. Retrieval metrics are indicators of a hidden property


Recall@k, MRR, and nDCG stand to "the retriever finds the relevant material" as a benchmark score stands to "the model reasons". RAG metrics says it directly: a metric "is not 'the truth'. It is a measurement proxy." [Retrieval reframing]({{< ref "ai-engineering/context/retrieval/evidence-workflows" >}}): "a hit is a candidate. Retrieval does not establish that it is true, applicable, or sufficient support for a claim", and index coverage, screening coverage, requirement support, and judged recall "have different denominators", which is a discriminant-validity point. [Retrieval design]({{< ref "ai-engineering/context/retrieval/retrieval-design" >}}): a click "becomes a relevance label only under a defined interpretation". The retrieval notes are a small, self-contained instance of the whole ladder, with relevance judgments as the trusted labels.

### 16. "Understanding" already has an operational proposal


[12]({{< ref "ai/design-research/01-foundations/12-research-terms-and-levels-of-analysis" >}}) proposes that a system understands a domain to the extent that its representations support prediction, intervention, counterfactuals, and transfer beyond seen cases, and that the definition is graded. It also gives every disputed term six fields, including "does not imply" and "evidence required to use the term", and sets explicit levels of analysis from model weights through model plus context, scaffold, and runtime to human plus AI plus institution, with the rule "claims must state which level they concern". That is the system boundary of [04]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/04-dispositions-as-response-profiles" >}}) as a ladder of its own. [13]({{< ref "ai/design-research/06-research-reports/13-research-method-and-claim-validation" >}}) turns the definition into tests. [01]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/01-what-is-a-capability-claim" >}}) calls understanding the hardest case. 13's warning that producing "I made this mistake because..." is evidence of self-explanation and not of metacognition is the mimic problem for the metacognition component of [07]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/07-reasoning-as-a-capability" >}}).

### 17. The reasoning profile has engineering counterparts


[07]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/07-reasoning-as-a-capability" >}})'s ten-item capability profile overlaps with the recipes in [checks]({{< ref "ai-engineering/causal-stack/checks" >}}) and the failure cases in eval failure modes. Robustness to irrelevant premises is the check "wording, position, and irrelevant material" and the invariance test "change irrelevant facts and expect the decision to stay the same". Calibration and abstention are the check "uncertainty, refusal, and fallback". Transfer is the perturbation set in [09]({{< ref "ai/design-research/01-foundations/09-verification-and-acceptance" >}}): paraphrase, reorder sources, change a domain value, run held-out examples. The counterfactual test, "change one clinically meaningful fact and expect the decision to change", is a direct test that the right causal path is in use (feature 7 of [04]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/04-dispositions-as-response-profiles" >}})).

### 18. EBSE is the outer loop


The EBSE five steps (answerable question, best evidence, critical appraisal, integration with context, evaluation of the outcome) are a loop around a study. [05]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/05-measuring-dispositions" >}})'s ten-step order (purpose, phenomenon, ontology, theory, hypotheses, operationalization, variation, measurement model, validation, use) is the inside of one study. The same is true of Wohlin's scoping, planning, execution, analysis, and presentation, quoted in [Running an experiment]({{< ref "engineering/evidence/running-experiments" >}}). The capability notes do the "critical appraisal" step for benchmarks (Chizhov, Reynolds, Dellibarda Varela in the [sources]({{< ref "ai/capabilities" >}})) without calling it that.

## Where the analogies break


- **Property versus decision.** The evaluation framework and causal stack exist to make product decisions, and they say a finding is not a decision. The capability notes exist to make a property claim, and they say a score is not the property. Both separate the evidence from what it is used for, but they are used for different things.
- **Observed behavior versus disposition.** [AI evaluation]({{< ref "ai-engineering/evaluation/framework/introduction" >}}) says "evaluation never shows everything the product could do" and restricts itself to what happened under tested conditions. [Working assumptions]({{< ref "ai-engineering/working-assumptions" >}}) defines intelligence as "an operational performance property, not a claim about the mechanism". These are deliberate refusals to go above rung 4. The capability notes go to rungs 5 and 6 and accept the burden. The disagreement is about how far to climb, not about the ladder.
- **Profile versus delta.** The methodology framework estimates deltas between versions under a fixed input regime. Dispositional measurement holds the system fixed and maps behavior over a range of contexts. One compares systems at a point. The other describes one system over a space. A capability study can still use a delta, for example "adding the graph flattens the decline" in [08]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/08-deriving-capabilities-from-tasks" >}}), but the delta is in the shape of a curve, not in a single average. The methodology framework's slice-conditional estimand \(\mu_s(c)\) becomes a point on a response curve when the slices are ordered along a demand.
- **Who the subject is.** EBSE studies tools in human hands, and its skill and lifecycle factors are about people and time. A capability study has no human subject unless the system boundary includes one. If it does, as in human-AI collaboration ([11]({{< ref "ai/design-research/06-research-reports/11-human-ai-collaboration-evaluation" >}}), which varies AI quality systematically and says joint performance cannot be inferred from the members), the EBSE threats come back.
- **Status.** The methodology framework is theoretical and untested. The EBSE note itself is a short generic summary. The protocol and methodological foundations are the substantial appraisal notes. The sandbox-first note is a research proposal with a bibliography. The capability notes are an account, not a tested protocol.

## The word "capability" in other notes


The capability notes use the word in one sense: an ability under conditions, inferred from behavior. Other notes use it in at least five other senses. Keeping them apart matters when following links.

| Sense | Where | Relation to this section |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ability under conditions** | [12]({{< ref "ai/design-research/01-foundations/12-research-terms-and-levels-of-analysis" >}}) ("ability of a system to perform a particular task under specified conditions"), [sandbox-first]({{< ref "ai/design/workflow-design/sandbox-first-capability-discovery-llm-agents" >}}), [uncertainty ladder]({{< ref "ai/product/discovery/uncertainty-ladder" >}}) ("a capability at bar, with means, on an input distribution"), [working assumptions]({{< ref "ai-engineering/working-assumptions" >}}) | the same sense; these notes should link here |
| **Product function or requirement** | [capability funnel]({{< ref "ai/product/operating-model/working-notes/capability-funnel" >}}) ("capabilities describe what that solution must do"), ideas: capabilities ("something an LLM does that can be built into many applications"), Anthropic's capability evals in empirical nature 2 | what the product must do, not what the model can do; the utility ladder replaced the funnel and avoids the word |
| **Scope of permitted action** | assistant types ("inform, recommend, prepare, or execute") | authority, not ability |
| **Object-capability token** | [06]({{< ref "ai/design-research/01-foundations/06-architectural-least-commitment-and-precedents" >}}), [08]({{< ref "ai/design-research/01-foundations/08-semantic-substrate-builder-method" >}}) | a security term; a homonym |
| **Organizational capability** | [evaluations]({{< ref "ai-engineering/approach/evaluations" >}}) ("a maintained capability for understanding how an AI product behaves"), evidence capability in [product improvement system]({{< ref "ai-engineering/evaluation/framework/product-improvement-system-revised" >}}) | a team's ability, not a system's |
| **Model feature flag** | [model gateway]({{< ref "ai-engineering/delivery/patterns/model-gateway" >}}) (vision, tool calling, JSON mode) | unrelated |

One line from [AI spec]({{< ref "ai-engineering/delivery/production-ready/ai-specific-definition" >}}) sits between the senses and is worth keeping: "Metrics are mechanisms; operational understanding is a capability." A metric is a thing you build; the understanding it is supposed to give is a property you have to show you have.

## Toward a protocol for capability studies


This section is a proposal, assembled from the notes above. It has not been tried. Each step names the note it comes from.

| Step | What to produce | From |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. **State the claim** | System, scenario family, threshold; the level of the claim on the inference ladder; which uncertainty rungs are open; which utility claim it serves | [04]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/04-dispositions-as-response-profiles" >}}), [00]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/00-the-inference-ladder" >}}), [uncertainty ladder]({{< ref "ai/product/discovery/uncertainty-ladder" >}}), [utility ladder]({{< ref "ai/product/operating-model/solution-utility-ladder" >}}), [13]({{< ref "ai/design-research/06-research-reports/13-research-method-and-claim-validation" >}}) §4 |
| 2. **Check prior evidence** | What existing benchmarks and studies show, appraised for validity | EBSE steps 2 and 3, [13]({{< ref "ai/design-research/06-research-reports/13-research-method-and-claim-validation" >}}) §5 item 1 (scoping review) |
| 3. **Fix the system boundary** | Model version, prompts, tools, sampling settings, scaffolding, budget, in the sandbox note's \(M, E, T, P, B\) form | [05]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/05-measuring-dispositions" >}}) step 1, [sandbox-first]({{< ref "ai/design/workflow-design/sandbox-first-capability-discovery-llm-agents" >}}), [protocol]({{< ref "ai-engineering/evaluation/theoretical-methodology/appraisal/evidence-protocol" >}}) item 3, C-plane in [experimental design]({{< ref "ai-engineering/evaluation/theoretical-methodology/framework/experimental-design-v1-1" >}}) |
| 4. **Hypothesize the demands** | Candidate \(\pi\), seeded from failure categories and from the construct's theory, each identifiable from the task itself | [05]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/05-measuring-dispositions" >}}) step 2, [failure understanding]({{< ref "ai-engineering/evaluation/framework/20-error-analysis" >}}), [user inputs]({{< ref "ai-engineering/evaluation/framework/10-user-inputs" >}}), [06]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/06-capability-constructs-and-validity" >}}) |
| 5. **Write competing explanations** | At least two, and the result that would tell them apart; the RQ6 list as a checklist of non-capability explanations | [13]({{< ref "ai/design-research/06-research-reports/13-research-method-and-claim-validation" >}}) §5 item 5, [claims]({{< ref "ai-engineering/causal-stack/claims" >}}), [research questions]({{< ref "ai-engineering/agents/agents-md/coding-agent-research-questions" >}}) |
| 6. **Operationalize and preregister** | Variables, scoring rule, analysis, and exclusions, fixed before running; cases written to the requirement, not around the model | [05]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/05-measuring-dispositions" >}}) step 3, [protocol]({{< ref "ai-engineering/evaluation/theoretical-methodology/appraisal/evidence-protocol" >}}) item 4, [data]({{< ref "ai-engineering/delivery/df-data" >}}) |
| 7. **Validate the ruler** | Evidence that the scorer or judge measures what the criterion says: false passes and failures, boundary cases, stability, insensitivity to irrelevant wording; failure recall, not agreement | [judgment and findings]({{< ref "ai-engineering/evaluation/framework/30-judgment-and-findings" >}}), eval failure modes, R-plane in [foundations]({{< ref "ai-engineering/evaluation/theoretical-methodology/framework/foundations-v1-3" >}}) |
| 8. **Vary, repeat, estimate** | Dense controlled variation of \(\pi\), repetitions per point (pass^k where all-or-nothing matters), \(\hat p(v \mid \pi, \theta)\) with uncertainty; repetitions kept apart from coverage | [05]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/05-measuring-dispositions" >}}) step 4, [estimation]({{< ref "ai-engineering/evaluation/theoretical-methodology/framework/estimation-v1-2" >}}) §5, [checks]({{< ref "ai-engineering/causal-stack/checks" >}}) |
| 9. **Appraise validity** | Content, convergent, discriminant, criterion; internal and conclusion validity; T1 to T6; run the mimic and mask instances of correspondence 5 as a checklist | [06]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/06-capability-constructs-and-validity" >}}), [validity]({{< ref "engineering/evidence/validity" >}}) |
| 10. **Intervene on mechanisms** (if the claim is mechanistic) | Ablations and oracle replacements on the same base model with everything else held fixed | [08]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/08-deriving-capabilities-from-tasks" >}}) §6, ReAct report |
| 11. **Test transport** | Naturalistic or held-out evidence on tasks not used to build the measurement; perturbation set | [design-science evaluation]({{< ref "ai-engineering/delivery/design-science-evaluation" >}}), criterion validity in [06]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/06-capability-constructs-and-validity" >}}), [09]({{< ref "ai/design-research/01-foundations/09-verification-and-acceptance" >}}) |
| 12. **Report** | Negative results, failures, the preserved experiment, the evidence source each number is over, and what would change the conclusion | [protocol]({{< ref "ai-engineering/evaluation/theoretical-methodology/appraisal/evidence-protocol" >}}) items 7, 8, 10, [judgment and findings]({{< ref "ai-engineering/evaluation/framework/30-judgment-and-findings" >}}) §measurements, [13]({{< ref "ai/design-research/06-research-reports/13-research-method-and-claim-validation" >}}) §5 item 9 and §6 |

## Gaps


- **No worked capability study.** [07]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/07-reasoning-as-a-capability" >}}) gives a capability profile for reasoning but no study run to the protocol above. The arithmetic example in [05]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/05-measuring-dispositions" >}}) is the smallest candidate. The sandbox-first note's experiments table is the nearest existing design.
- **No response curve in the engineering notes.** Difficulty appears as challenge sets, coverage categories, slices, or a confound, never as an ordered variable held independent of the model and swept. Correspondence 8 lists the fragments.
- **Finks are absent.** No engineering note has a concept of evaluation awareness or test detection.
- **Construct-validity vocabulary is absent from the engineering notes.** The ideas appear as evaluator validation, the utility ladder, and "different denominators", but the words construct, nomological, convergent, and discriminant do not.
- **Systematic reviews and the empirical standards have no notes.** [EBSE]({{< ref "engineering/evidence/ebse" >}}) and the [evidence section]({{< ref "engineering/evidence" >}}) cover the five steps, replication and synthesis, and the study methods, but there are no notes on systematic reviews or on the SIGSOFT empirical standards themselves.
- **Statistics is scattered.** Sample size, power, and repeated measures appear only in the methodology framework's estimation spec.
- **Understanding is split.** [01]({{< ref "ai/capabilities/01-what-is-a-capability-claim" >}}), [12]({{< ref "ai/design-research/01-foundations/12-research-terms-and-levels-of-analysis" >}}), and [13]({{< ref "ai/design-research/06-research-reports/13-research-method-and-claim-validation" >}}) treat the same question and are only partly linked.
- **Missing links.** The evaluation framework notes do not link to the capability notes; only their index does.
