## Status


The initial comparison is complete in [research-1-notes.md]({{< ref "ai/product/product-model/comparison/ai-era-discovery" >}}). It covers:

- the core vocabulary and conceptual mapping between both frameworks;
- product risks versus assumption types;
- continuous discovery, opportunity solution trees, and empowered teams;
- differences in role accountability and customer-contact cadence;
- discovery evidence, prototyping, and the discovery-to-delivery boundary;
- how AI changes product risk, prototyping, evaluation, and production readiness; and
- a proposed integrated AI-era discovery model.

The next stage should not repeat that survey. It should investigate unresolved operating-model questions, boundary conditions, and empirical claims.

## Research objective


Develop a deeper comparison of how the two frameworks guide real product decisions, especially where:

1. they operate at different organizational levels;
2. they prescribe different practices or decision rights;
3. their concepts appear compatible but the connection is underspecified; or
4. their recommendations depend on context or lack strong empirical validation.

The central unresolved question is:

> SVPG explains how organizations select strategically important problems, while Torres explains how teams explore customer opportunities and solution assumptions. How do those two decision systems connect, and what happens when they disagree?

## Concepts for further comparative research

### 1. Strategy-to-discovery translation


Compare SVPG's `vision -> strategy -> objective -> problem` chain with Torres's `business outcome -> product outcome -> opportunity` chain.

Research questions:

- How does company strategy become a bounded discovery space for a team?
- What strategic context must leadership provide?
- How much latitude should remain in the outcome or problem assignment?
- Can the two chains be combined without duplicating concepts or creating extra layers?

### 2. Problem-selection authority


Investigate who determines the problem being solved: leadership, the product team, customer research, or some combination.

Research questions:

- What happens when an opportunity solution tree reveals a more important problem than the one assigned through strategy?
- When may a team challenge or replace an assigned problem?
- How should leadership distinguish productive reframing from loss of strategic focus?

### 3. Opportunity and problem prioritization


Compare Torres's opportunity-selection criteria with SVPG's strategic focus, opportunity assessment, and biggest-risk thinking.

Research questions:

- Do the methods select the same work from an identical body of evidence?
- How do customer importance, market size, strategic fit, tractability, and unresolved risk interact?
- How should teams handle related opportunities that do not fit neatly into one branch?

### 4. Generative versus evaluative discovery


Torres's interviews continuously generate and refine opportunities. SVPG often presents product discovery as finding a solution to an already selected problem, while placing generative insights partly in product strategy.

Research questions:

- Where should problem discovery live?
- How much discovery capacity should be reserved for unassigned opportunities?
- How should a team capture important opportunities that fall outside its current objective?

### 5. Product outcomes and causal models


Compare Torres's business outcomes, product outcomes, and traction metrics with SVPG's team objectives, key results, analytics, and business results.

Research questions:

- How does a team establish that a product outcome is a real leading indicator of a business result?
- How should weak, delayed, or disputed causal relationships be handled?
- When is a traction metric an appropriate optimization objective rather than a disguised output?
- How should contributions from several teams or business functions be attributed?

### 6. Product judgment and structured decision-making


SVPG emphasizes product sense and judgment. Torres emphasizes repeatable habits, visible reasoning, and structured comparison.

Research questions:

- Can structured habits develop product judgment, or do they mainly support people who already possess it?
- Where should a team rely on judgment when evidence is incomplete?
- How can organizations evaluate and develop judgment without making it an unchallengeable appeal to seniority?

### 7. Decision and stopping rules


Both frameworks identify uncertainty and encourage evidence gathering, but neither provides a universal formula for deciding when evidence is sufficient.

Research questions:

- When should a team build, abandon, revisit, release, or continue testing a solution?
- How should evidence thresholds vary with reversibility, investment, and consequence of failure?
- How should contradictory qualitative, behavioral, technical, and business evidence be resolved?

### 8. Opportunity Solution Tree validity


Investigate the OST as a representation of knowledge and a decision aid rather than assuming that visualization necessarily improves decisions.

Research questions:

- Does an OST improve decision quality, alignment, recall, or learning speed?
- When might a tree oversimplify interconnected needs or imply unsupported certainty?
- Which rules determine whether two observations are separate opportunities, parent and child opportunities, or evidence for the same opportunity?

### 9. Customer interviewing models


Compare Torres's story-based interviewing and weekly cadence with SVPG's customer interviews, qualitative value testing, reference customers, and customer-discovery programs.

Research questions:

- Which interview methods are generative, evaluative, or both?
- What constitutes a meaningful customer touchpoint?
- How do recruiting constraints and buyer-user separation change the practice?
- How should interview evidence be combined with observed behavior and analytics?

### 10. Ideation and alternative generation


Compare Torres's structured ideation and multiple-solution requirement with SVPG's product insights, technology insights, prototypes, and product sense.

Research questions:

- How many alternatives are enough for a useful comparison?
- What level of diversity between alternatives matters?
- How should technology-enabled possibilities enter an opportunity-first process?
- How can cheap AI generation avoid producing many superficial variations of one idea?

### 11. Discovery cadence and capacity allocation


Torres prescribes weekly customer contact. SVPG advocates continuous discovery but organizes activity around the risks being addressed.

Research questions:

- Is weekly contact universally useful or a context-sensitive default?
- How much capacity should go to interviews, tests, prototypes, delivery, and production learning?
- Should cadence vary by the maturity of the problem, product, or team?

### 12. Outcome measurement after release


Compare Torres's impact measurement and discovery-delivery cycles with SVPG's analytics, delivery, and product optimization.

Research questions:

- When does learning from a released product become discovery again?
- How should post-release results update opportunities, assumptions, and product risks?
- When should weak outcome movement trigger a new solution, a new opportunity, or a revised causal model?

### 13. Discovery versus optimization


Both frameworks recognize a difference between open-ended discovery and improving a known workflow.

Research questions:

- What observable criteria distinguish discovery from optimization?
- When should a team switch modes?
- Can optimization expose a deeper discovery problem, and how should that transition be represented?

### 14. Team decision rights


The initial research established a real difference between Torres's joint accountability and SVPG's primary risk ownership. The remaining issue is how decisions are actually made.

Research questions:

- Who decides when product management, design, and engineering disagree?
- Does primary stewardship improve clarity or recreate functional silos?
- Does joint accountability increase shared understanding or produce consensus-driven delay?

### 15. Stakeholder participation and commitments


Compare Torres's work-visibility approach with SVPG's stakeholder management, viability work, and high-integrity commitments.

Research questions:

- When should stakeholders participate in discovery rather than review its conclusions?
- How can teams surface viability constraints early without turning stakeholders into solution approvers?
- How should commitments be made while major discovery uncertainty remains?

### 16. Knowledge representation and transfer


Compare the OST as a living knowledge model with SVPG's discovery narratives, shared learnings, and strategic insights.

Research questions:

- Which artifacts retain context when knowledge moves between teams?
- How should superseded assumptions and negative results be preserved?
- What should be communicated upward to leadership or sideways to other teams?
- Can a tree represent uncertainty and disagreement, not merely the current preferred interpretation?

### 17. Scaling across teams and portfolios


Both frameworks are clearest at the single-team level, although SVPG provides more organizational and portfolio structure.

Research questions:

- How should outcomes, opportunity spaces, strategic problems, and evidence be coordinated across teams?
- Can several teams share an opportunity space without weakening ownership?
- How should cross-team dependencies and platform work appear in the discovery model?

### 18. Framework adoption and transformation


Compare Torres's start-small-and-iterate approach with SVPG's pilot teams and product-model transformation.

Research questions:

- What organizational preconditions does each approach assume?
- Which practices can a team adopt without leadership or structural change?
- When does local adoption succeed, plateau, or become discovery theater?

### 19. Contextual boundaries


Test both frameworks outside the typical autonomous software-product-team setting.

Contexts to investigate:

- B2B and enterprise products;
- platforms and APIs;
- internal products;
- regulated and safety-critical environments;
- hardware and mixed physical-digital products;
- early startups and product-market-fit work; and
- mature products dominated by optimization and operational constraints.

Research questions:

- Which recommendations are stable principles?
- Which are techniques that require adaptation?
- What substitutes are appropriate when weekly end-user access or fast experimentation is impossible?

### 20. Market discovery and product-market fit


SVPG distinguishes market discovery, product discovery, and product-market fit. Torres's framework begins with an outcome and customer opportunity space but does not emphasize the same boundary.

Research questions:

- How does market selection constrain an OST?
- Can an opportunity space help discover a market rather than operate inside a predefined one?
- How do the frameworks change before and after product-market fit?

### 21. Ethical decision governance


The initial research resolves where ethics appears in each taxonomy. The next question is operational governance.

Research questions:

- Who defines acceptable harm?
- What evidence is required before accepting residual ethical or safety risk?
- Who has authority to stop or constrain a solution?
- How should post-release harms update discovery and product strategy?

### 22. Empirical effectiveness


Separate author recommendations, practitioner reports, and independently validated evidence.

Research questions:

- What evidence shows that weekly interviews, OSTs, multiple alternatives, assumption decomposition, empowered teams, or risk ownership improve product outcomes?
- Which claims are supported by controlled studies, observational studies, case reports, or only expert experience?
- What measurable outcomes would allow a fair comparison of the two frameworks?

## Recommended first research batch


Begin with these six concepts:

1. strategy-to-discovery translation;
2. problem-selection authority;
3. opportunity and problem prioritization;
4. generative versus evaluative discovery;
5. product outcomes and causal models; and
6. product judgment and structured decision-making.

These concepts form one coherent investigation of how organizational strategy connects to team-level discovery. Research them together before moving into techniques, scaling, or special contexts.

## Suggested research sequence


1. Establish each author's definitions using primary sources.
2. Map the complete decision chain in each framework from company intent to shipped product and measured outcome.
3. Identify explicit decision owners, inputs, outputs, and feedback loops at every stage.
4. Separate direct disagreement from differences in scope, terminology, or level of abstraction.
5. Find concrete cases that expose the consequences of each difference.
6. Check important effectiveness claims against independent research where available.
7. Produce a practical synthesis only after documenting the authors' original positions.

## Expected output


Create a second comparison note that contains:

- an executive summary of the new findings;
- a side-by-side model of the strategy-to-discovery chain;
- definitions and source-supported positions from both authors;
- genuine agreements, disagreements, and underspecified connections;
- evidence quality and limitations for major claims;
- implications for teams using one or both frameworks;
- unresolved questions for later research; and
- a source list that clearly separates primary author material from independent evidence.

Avoid re-explaining the AI risk overlay unless a finding materially changes the conclusions in the initial research.

## Research guardrails


- Treat author material as evidence of what each author recommends, not evidence that the recommendation is effective.
- Prefer primary author sources when attributing a position.
- Use independent research for effectiveness, causal, or prevalence claims.
- Preserve differences in scope. Do not manufacture disagreement when one framework is describing organizational strategy and the other is describing team practice.
- Distinguish canonical concepts from the researcher's proposed synthesis.
- Record publication dates where positions may have evolved.
- Do not assume that a shared term has the same definition in both frameworks.
- Do not treat value versus desirability as a major theoretical disagreement without new evidence; the initial research already establishes their substantial overlap.
- Do not repeat the initial AI survey unless later evidence changes its conclusions.
