## Units of analysis and causal attribution


The sequence from model weights to a human, AI, and institutional system identifies possible subjects of a claim. It is not a complete ontology, a required architecture, or an established causal sequence. Participants, processes, and environments may overlap or depend on a study's chosen boundary.

A study should identify the observed system and the state that persists across a relevant boundary. A change in a whole configured system does not by itself locate the cause in the model, and a useful learning example does not establish a general test of learning.

### Working conventions and Alice assumptions


The narrow task-performance definition of intelligence is a chosen convention, not a consensus definition. Separating task competence from understanding or agency can clarify a claim without proving which cognitive properties a particular model possesses.

The discussion uses viability, mattering, relational meaning, and internally regulated activity to interpret the Alice proposal. The [Alice scope note]({{< ref "ai/design-research/00-overview/08-collaborative-computation-and-alice-research-scope" >}}) keeps the emergence of cognition separate from collaboration among existing participants. Importing the dictionary does not require a collaborative runtime to provide organism-like viability or intrinsic meaning.

Definitions alone do not establish empirical claims about LLMs, human cognition, or the cost of capabilities. The [research-method proposal]({{< ref "ai/design-research/06-research-reports/13-research-method-and-claim-validation" >}}) describes possible evidence requirements, while the retained dictionary remains a proposal to examine.

### Preserved revisions


The initial reply includes a general-intelligence definition. The narrowed reply suggests avoiding that definition initially because task distributions, novelty, transfer, and generality remain difficult to specify. Both positions remain visible.

The initial learning definition requires persistent modification that improves future behavior. The v0.1 draft (archived source 006) and v0.2 draft (archived source 007) instead describe persistent change in behavior or capability. The wording was not normalized into a single agreed definition.

The initial understanding definition explicitly requires grounded concepts. The narrowed functional definition does not repeat that requirement, although its Alice examples still emphasize causal interaction. The v0.2 draft leaves the necessary degree of grounding open. A later comparison should retain the differences instead of treating grounding as a settled necessary condition of understanding.

### Grounding, autonomy, and the dependency diagram


Causal grounding concerns how representations connect to what they represent. Source support concerns whether evidence supports an answer, and common ground concerns sufficient shared understanding between participants. The [participant-profile comments]({{< ref "ai/design-research/01-foundations/11-participant-profiles-and-work-allocation-comments" >}}#grounding-common-ground-persistence-and-learning) discuss related distinctions, and the meanings should not be silently substituted for one another.

The source distinguishes unattended execution from internally regulated activity. A system can grant permission to act without establishing the stronger autonomy described by the dictionary. A learning or agency claim also needs a stated unit of analysis, since persistent state may belong to the whole configured system rather than the model.

The closing diagram places grounding, understanding, agency, autonomy, and meaning in a proposed sequence. The source explicitly says its causal order is unestablished. The diagram and warning remain together, and the arrangement establishes no necessary or sufficient relation among the concepts.
