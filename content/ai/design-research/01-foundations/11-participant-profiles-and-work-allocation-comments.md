## Why the initial and revised models stay together


The opening dialogue asks whether participant properties can provide a basis for deriving design approaches. Its comparisons of humans, LLMs, and software lead to augmentation, delegation, verification, constrained generation, mixed initiative, escalation, and reversible autonomy.

The final reply revises the profiles and expands the dimensions. Keeping the complete revisions with the first model makes the changes visible, including the more symmetrical account of human fallibility and the separation of capability, competence, grounding, agency, and authority.

The intervening [framing dialogue]({{< ref "ai/design-research/02-human-ai-collaboration/01-augmenting-human-work" >}}#from-conversational-interfaces-to-collaborative-work) and [practices guide]({{< ref "ai/design-research/02-human-ai-collaboration/08-collaborative-system-design-practices" >}}) remain separate because they concern the product's design approach and practical questions.

The existing [participant-neutral architecture]({{< ref "ai/design-research/01-foundations/04-participant-neutral-system-model" >}}) describes shared state and checked contributions. A profile can inform that architecture without making the profile and architecture the same model.

## Profiles depend on tasks and conditions


The tables summarize proposed tendencies. They do not supply measurements showing that every human, LLM, or software component has those properties under every condition.

A later allocation needs the actual task, participant configuration, available information, and evidence of competence. Statements about cheap processing, high precision, social judgment, or reliability should not replace task-specific evaluation.

The revised description acknowledges human errors, while conventional software's reliability depends on its specification and inputs. Assigning verification to a different participant can address some failure modes without proving that the verifier is correct or that errors are independent.

The proposed dimension lists are not a validated scale. Adding dimensions does not show that the list is complete, and some dimensions overlap or require further definitions.

## Participant properties and assigned authority


The revised reply distinguishes capabilities from authority and accountability. The ability to generate an action is different from permission to execute it.

Authority depends on the role and policy in the configured system. The accountability rows describe proposed human and organizational responsibilities, rather than establishing a legal conclusion about every participant or use.

The source also moves between an isolated model invocation and a participant embedded in persistent software. A later specification should state which unit is being described, since state, tools, triggers, and permissions can be supplied by the surrounding system.

## Dictionary context and autonomy


A matching dictionary dialogue (archived source 005) was read as context. It separates task performance, understanding, grounding, agency, and autonomy. The source does not name a path or version, so the matching note is not confirmed as the exact document supplied during the original exchange.

The dictionary uses autonomy to discuss where regulation of continued activity originates. The practices use autonomy to discuss how much action can proceed without another approval. A system can permit unattended execution without settling the dictionary's stronger question.

The source's generation and generativity terms concern constructing representations and candidates. The [constrained-generativity note]({{< ref "ai/design-research/01-foundations/01-constrained-generativity" >}}) develops a separate constraint-and-composition argument. The shared word does not establish a common measure.

## Grounding, common ground, persistence, and learning


The profile's grounding concerns how a representation connects to what it represents. The collaboration model's common ground concerns sufficient shared understanding between participants. Access to the same evidence can support both questions without making them identical.

The revised dimensions group learning and persistence. Storing state preserves information, while a learning claim concerns changes in future behavior or competence. A design should state which property it needs instead of treating memory as proof of learning.

## From profiles to design approaches


The derivations are proposed reasons for allocating work. They do not prove that a specific allocation improves the combined outcome or that deterministic constraints establish the correctness of generated content.
