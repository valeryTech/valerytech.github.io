## Why the report is split


The executive summary describes the report as a whole, so it stays with the complete original bibliography. The [pattern catalog and selection guidance]({{< ref "ai/design-research/02-human-ai-collaboration/06-human-ai-collaboration-patterns" >}}#practitioner-and-research-synthesis) extend the existing pattern note. The [evaluation framework]({{< ref "ai/design-research/06-research-reports/11-human-ai-collaboration-evaluation" >}}) concerns whether a person and AI can work together under the stated conditions. It has a separate note because interaction tests differ from the runtime and evidence checks elsewhere in the collection. Each catalog entry still contains its own suggested tests.

The complete workflow stays with the concluding model. Removing individual steps to place them under separate patterns would obscure the example's purpose, which is to show how responsibility can change within one task.

## A proposed synthesis rather than a settled taxonomy


The report separates modes, transitions, and mechanics. The proposed six modes describe how information, work, and judgment are distributed. Handoff and escalation describe changes in responsibility.

The earlier dialogue lists handoff alongside the other patterns. Both versions remain in the collection, and the [pattern comments]({{< ref "ai/design-research/02-human-ai-collaboration/06-human-ai-collaboration-patterns-comments" >}}#later-report-taxonomy) explain the change. Reassembly does not establish which classification should govern a future design.

The report compares a decision-support taxonomy, creative collaboration research, teamwork accounts, interface guidelines, and product examples. The sources do not necessarily study the same tasks or use the same unit of analysis. Similar terms therefore do not establish identical definitions or a common empirical basis.

The source's claims about a dominant interaction pattern, the number of reviewed papers, and agreement across traditions have not been checked against the cited works. The report does not record a reproducible search and coding procedure for its own synthesis.

## Product examples and empirical claims


Product descriptions illustrate the report's proposed categories. They do not demonstrate that every use of a product follows that category, or that the described controls provide effective oversight.

Descriptions of plans, permissions, review gates, autonomy, and interruptions may depend on a product version or setting. The reported percentages and observations remain source claims, rather than measurements confirmed during this editing session. A later verification pass should check the original study conditions as well as current product behavior.

The report's references are retained for inspection. Their presence does not verify publication details, URL availability, or support for each nearby claim.

The original bibliography is moved once into the synthesis note. Clearly labelled local copies provide definitions for the unchanged footnote labels in the other destinations. The citation index in the synthesis note refers to all original entries, including entries cited only elsewhere.

## Relation to the design system


The material could help describe who performs a task, who supplies information, and who can judge or authorize a contribution. It does not select an implementation or establish a complete theory of complex systems.

A later design needs definitions for the task, current allocation, authority boundary, and conditions for changing that allocation. The arrangement makes no research or implementation decision.
