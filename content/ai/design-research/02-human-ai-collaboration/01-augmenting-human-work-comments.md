### Preserving a claim and developing a claim differ


The proposed prompt distinguishes faithful rewrites from alternatives that extend an idea. Improving terminology can also change the meaning, scope, or certainty of a claim.

A later design should show proposed changes in meaning and let the writer accept or reject them. If the writer deliberately changes the claim, the resulting revision should remain distinguishable from a rewrite of the earlier claim.

Preserving intended meaning does not establish factual correctness. The requirement to avoid invented facts states desired behavior, but the sketch provides no evaluation showing that the requirement is met.

### Task context can change during writing


The sketch proposes modelling the user and names several writing tasks. It does not define the information to retain about the writer or how the writer can correct an inferred domain.

The examples describe intent and required context as changing during collaboration. A later design should distinguish an accepted change in intent from an unsupported assumption by the model.

The writing case concerns what a text means to its writer and intended reader, so the related problems should remain distinguishable.
